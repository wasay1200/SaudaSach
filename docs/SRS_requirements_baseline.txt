1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose of Document
This SRS specifies the functional, non-functional, interface, data and acceptance requirements of SaudaSach version 1.0. It is the basis for the Software Design Specification (SDS), implementation, testing and academic review. The release includes a React Native Android application and reproducible offline research scripts. It does not assert that an untested model achieves a particular accuracy.
1.2 Intended Audience
1.3 Definitions, Acronyms and Abbreviations
1.4 Document Conventions
The template uses Arial, 13-point section headings and 11-point body text. FR-### identifies functional requirements and NFR-### non-functional requirements. UC-USER-## and UC-RES-## identify app-user and researcher use cases. TC-F-### and TC-N-### identify verification cases. BR-## identifies business rules, whose enforcing requirements appear in Section 9.
“Shall” denotes a testable requirement at its stated priority. Must requirements define release acceptance; Should requirements are measured targets whose shortfalls require a documented disposition. Could features are optional; Won’t items are excluded. Research targets are hypotheses rather than completed results. Every normative FR and NFR appears in Sections 8 and 9.
2. Overall Description
2.1 Project Background
Synthetic speech and voice conversion can imitate human voices and complicate assessment of suspicious audio. Urdu research includes the specialized dataset by Munir et al. [1] and its Tacotron/VITS corpus [2], RVC-based Urdu detection by Ahmad et al. [3], and comparisons of compact and Whisper-based models by Owais et al. [4]. These works motivate a controlled Urdu study rather than a claim that Urdu detection is unstudied.
Short degraded-audio detection is also studied by S-MGAA [5]. SaudaSach therefore investigates whether varied noise and compression training improves a compact Urdu detector over an otherwise equivalent baseline, and documents the accuracy, storage and device-latency trade-offs. It is an empirical extension using established techniques, not a claim of the first real-time detector.
2.2 Problem Statement
A model that classifies clean research clips may perform differently on compressed voice notes, short speech and background noise. Published accuracy values obtained on different datasets cannot establish comparative superiority. The project needs an accessible audio-checking workflow and controlled evidence showing how its trained models behave under matched clean, degraded and, where feasible, generator-held-out tests. False accusations caused by genuine speech being flagged must be measured explicitly.
2.3 Product Perspective
SaudaSach is a standalone Android research prototype. A user imports a voice note or makes a consented in-app recording; analysis uses a bundled local model. The offline workstation prepares data, trains models, evaluates variants and exports a verified bundle. Android supplies file selection, audio decoding, sharing and microphone permissions. No proprietary deepfake-detection API is required. Figure 1 separates runtime data from offline research data.
Figure 1. Context diagram for the application and offline research environment
2.4 Product Functions
Import supported audio from Android storage or a share action and record audio in the app.
Validate and normalize audio, run the local detector and show an interpretable outcome with limitations.
Delete local analysis data and access help without account registration.
Audit and partition Urdu data; train baseline and augmented detectors; evaluate detection and deployment trade-offs; package the selected model.
2.5 Project Scope
In scope: Android-first React Native and TypeScript interface; local model inference; file import and supported Android audio sharing; in-app recording; three-outcome result display; no persistent raw-audio history; a compact log-mel CNN as the primary neural detector; an MFCC-statistics SVM reference; optional causal TCN comparison; grouped data manifests; class-balanced noise/compression augmentation; documented clean/degraded evaluation; and an evaluated quantization variant where export is feasible.
Not in scope: intercepting cellular or WhatsApp calls; monitoring WhatsApp in the background; identifying a person; certifying truthfulness or fraud; guaranteed detection of every generator; partially manipulated segment localization; text transcription; iOS delivery; production cloud services; continuous public retraining; and autonomous financial or law-enforcement actions. Rolling live-microphone scoring is a stretch feature, not a release dependency. Sharing uses Android’s share mechanism, not a WhatsApp API integration.
2.6 Project Objectives
OBJ-01: Deliver a local Android workflow from supported audio selection or recording to an outcome and guidance, verified through the Must functional tests.
OBJ-02: Reproduce a compact baseline and evaluate whether varied noise and compression training improves balanced accuracy on a predeclared degraded test suite. A proposed target is +5 percentage points with no more than a 2-point clean-audio loss; success is not presumed.
OBJ-03: Report EER, FPR, class recalls and uncertainty, separating familiar and held-out conditions. A held-out-generator experiment is conditional on suitable independent data.
OBJ-04: Measure exported model size, preprocessing plus inference latency and runtime memory on a declared Android device; investigate quantization without concealing detection losses.
OBJ-05: Support informed use through inconclusive results, privacy controls, clear limits and a small observed usability study.

2.9 Operating Environment
The application targets Android 10 or later on ARM64 hardware with a microphone and at least 4 GB RAM as the initial test profile. Release testing uses one declared physical reference phone and at least one additional supported Android configuration. Initial limits are 3–60 seconds per imported file and 20 MiB; recording stops at 60 seconds. The analysis pipeline uses mono 16 kHz PCM and at least 2 seconds of detected speech. Model-score aggregation uses 2-second windows with a 0.5-second step for files long enough to contain those windows.
Training runs on a development workstation with a supported Python/PyTorch environment and periodic university or hosted GPU access. No guaranteed GPU quota is assumed. Runtime inference is local and works without internet after installation. Source-code and consented research storage remain separate from app runtime data.
2.10 Design and Implementation Constraints
React Native and TypeScript form the app layer; onnxruntime-react-native executes the selected neural export. A small Kotlin module may provide audio decoding, recording or feature extraction if required for parity.
Training uses Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn and audio tools such as librosa and FFmpeg. Exact versions and licenses are pinned in the release environment manifest after the integration pilot.
Compute limits favor small networks and bounded experiment sets. Training a speech foundation model from scratch is excluded. Detector weights are trained by the team; evaluation baselines may reimplement published architectures.
Android input-audio restrictions [6] limit the project to user-supplied audio and in-app microphone capture. The app cannot promise access to another application’s or cellular call audio.
The model output is an acoustic synthetic-speech assessment. Unsupported languages, excessive degradation and unfamiliar generators can lead to errors.
2.11 Assumptions and Dependencies
Assumptions: the team can access licensed Urdu audio, obtain explicit consent for any new recordings or cloning, and use at least one physical Android device. Suitably grouped source metadata is available or can be conservatively reconstructed. Missing metadata reduces the strength of speaker-independent claims and is reported.
Dependencies: the CSALT-LUMS corpus and its terms [2]; supported Android codecs and URI permissions; React Native, ONNX Runtime and Python training tools [7]–[9]; intermittent training compute; and, only if feasible, consented supplementary voice-conversion generation. Internet is needed to obtain tools and research data but not for the core installed app workflow. No paid third-party detection API, backend availability guarantee or external service rate limit is part of runtime operation.
2.12 Business Rules
2.13 Legal, Ethical and Regulatory Requirements
The project records source permissions and complies with each corpus license; the CSALT-LUMS repository identifies noncommercial conditions [2]. Dataset redistribution is limited to what the relevant license and participant permission allow. New collection and cloning use written, purpose-specific consent, restricted access and the withdrawal procedure in Section 6. NFR-012 makes this auditable. Institutional ethics and similarity-review procedures apply before collection or submission as required by the department; this SRS does not represent an ethics approval.
The system provides neither a legal authenticity certificate nor financial advice. Fairness is assessed through separately reported genuine Urdu and Urdu–English code-switched samples when sufficient labelled recordings exist. A small convenience sample cannot establish protection for the general population. Synthetic generation is limited to authorized research data and is not exposed as an app feature.
3. External Interface Requirements
Figure 1 defines the system boundary. The following interface contracts are verified by the functional and non-functional requirements rather than depending on a cloud detector.
3.1 User Interfaces
The home screen offers Import Audio, Record Audio and Help. The recording screen exposes permission rationale, elapsed time, Stop and Cancel. The review screen shows filename or recording label, duration and Analyze. The processing screen shows progress and Cancel. The result screen shows the outcome, synthetic-evidence score where available, model version, analyzed duration and guidance; Start Again and Delete return control to the user. A score is not labelled a probability unless calibration has been evaluated.
Outcomes are “Synthetic speech suspected,” “No strong synthetic evidence,” and “Inconclusive.” The second outcome does not certify authenticity. Errors identify the problem and a recovery action, such as selecting supported audio or recording more speech. English is the release interface language; Urdu help is a Should requirement. Font scaling, screen-reader labels and text-plus-icon outcomes are covered by NFR-015 and NFR-016. Detailed wireframes belong in the SDS.
3.2 Hardware Interfaces
Android supplies microphone capture and local file access. The app requests microphone permission only on recording entry and uses system file selection or received content URIs instead of unrestricted storage access. A phone without an available microphone can still import audio. Speaker playback, external headsets and telephony capture are not required interfaces. TC-F-002, TC-F-003 and TC-F-004 exercise these boundaries.
3.3 Software Interfaces
3.4 Communication Interfaces
The core app uses Android intents and local module calls, not HTTP, WebSocket, email or SMS services. No audio upload endpoint exists in this release. Research scripts use UTF-8 CSV/JSON manifests and local files; the model bundle contains ONNX weights and JSON preprocessing/threshold metadata. Development downloads and repository synchronization occur outside user analysis. Any future server-assisted interface requires a reviewed scope and consent change.

6. Data Requirements
The app uses transient session data rather than a public account database. Research datasets, provenance and model artifacts are maintained in a separate restricted workspace. Detailed schemas and storage layout belong in SDS Section 5.
Data retention, backup and privacy: app-owned raw audio, decoded PCM and features are transient and removed after analysis, cancellation or failure. Startup cleanup removes remnants from interrupted sessions before the next check. The app keeps only the current result in memory; it does not delete the user’s original file. Offline help, preferences and model weights remain until app removal. Temporary data are excluded from Android backups.
Research audio and identity-linked consent records are encrypted at rest and restricted to authorized team members. The proposed retention endpoint is six months after final project acceptance, subject to contributor permission and institutional instructions; longer retention requires a documented basis and renewed permission where needed. A contributor may request withdrawal before the final training freeze; the team removes eligible samples and derived variants and reruns affected training where feasible. Once weights or aggregate reports are released, individual influence cannot be guaranteed removable, and the consent form explains this limit. Code, split manifests without identifying information and permitted artifacts are backed up at each milestone; raw audio is not placed in a public repository.
7. User Documentation
The release documentation supports both app operation and reproduction of the research evidence.
User guide: installation, supported audio, recording, import/share workflow, outcome interpretation, deletion and privacy.
Offline help: English instructions and, if FR-016 is delivered, Urdu outcome and privacy explanations.
Troubleshooting guide: permissions, unsupported codecs, silence, corruption, insufficient speech and model-load errors.
Researcher guide: environment setup, data provenance, split generation, augmentation, training, threshold selection, evaluation and export.
Model card and evaluation report: training coverage, excluded cases, metrics, false positives, missing experiments, device profile and quantization trade-offs.
Consent and data-management materials for supplemental recording or cloning; deployment/build instructions and test evidence index.
8. Acceptance Criteria
The release is ready for academic acceptance when every Must requirement below passes with recorded evidence, no unresolved critical crash or privacy defect remains, all Should/Could items have an achieved/missed/deferred disposition, and the assigned supervisor reviews the results. Requirements marked Planned in Section 9 have not been tested by this document’s preparation. Final sign-off is recorded in the front matter.
Research completion requires executing and reporting the frozen protocol even if an improvement target is missed. The report can claim improvement only for comparisons supported by measured results and uncertainty. Before final-test access, the team and supervisor confirm the proposed +5-point robustness target, 2-point clean tolerance, quantization target, test corpus and reference phone. Subsequent target changes require a revision rather than silent retrospective adjustment.

Appendices
Appendix A Evaluation protocol
The mandatory study compares an ordinary compact CNN with the same architecture trained using varied noise and compression, plus an SVM reference. The split, training budget and checkpoint-selection rule are matched where applicable. An optional causal TCN is a separate architecture comparison. At least three seeds are run for the primary CNN comparison. Thresholds and inconclusive rules are selected using development data and frozen before final tests.
The proposed augmentation suite uses Opus and GSM encode/decode paths and separate environmental-noise recordings at 20, 10 and 5 dB SNR. The pilot verifies codec availability, intelligibility and feasibility before conditions are frozen. Both classes receive matched transformation distributions. These are controlled channel simulations, not exact reproductions of every WhatsApp or cellular processing chain. Test noise recordings do not appear in training; at least one degradation configuration is reserved as unfamiliar. All variants of a source remain in its group.
Results separate clean, noisy, compressed and combined conditions and include per-condition metrics and counts rather than only a pooled average. The primary balanced-accuracy comparison uses fixed validation-selected binary thresholds on all eligible clips. The three-outcome UI is evaluated separately with decision coverage and errors among decided clips, preventing rejection of difficult cases from inflating the primary score. EER uses all eligible continuous scores. FPR is reported on genuine Urdu and separately on available Urdu–English samples; its uncertainty is stated. No held-out-generator claim is made without a genuine independent generator family and matched genuine controls.
Device measurements include file decoding, resampling, feature extraction, inference and aggregation, with cold model load reported separately. They exclude the time spent receiving or recording the audio. A short input window is an evidence duration, not an inference-latency measurement. Quantized and full-precision exports are tested on the same recordings and runtime settings. Missing or failed experiments remain visible in the final report.
Appendix B Requirement verification fixtures
The fixture set covers WAV PCM, AAC in M4A and Ogg Opus on the supported device profile; stereo and alternate sample rates; silence and low-speech audio; duration/size boundaries; inaccessible URIs; malformed files; denied microphone permission; interrupted recording; repeated Analyze taps; cancellation; restart cleanup; invalid model checksums; feature parity; and scores on either side of both decision thresholds. Noise and generator evaluation recordings are independent of implementation unit fixtures. TC IDs and observed outcomes are retained with the tested build and model hash.
